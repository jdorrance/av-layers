## 3. First implement the input/science contract
### Acquire only fields the three layers consume
GFS recipe: surface terrain `HGT`, surface `PRES`, 2 m `TMP` and one admitted 2 m humidity representation, `HPBL`, `SHTFL`, `LHTFL` and total precipitation (`APCP`). Aloft fetch `HGT` and `TMP` only at the 21 levels `1000,975,950,925,900,875,850,825,800,775,750,725,700,675,650,625,600,550,500,450,400` hPa. Resolve the 15 primary levels (`1000,975,950,925,900,850,800,750,700,650,600,550,500,450,400` hPa) plus surface fields through `pgrb2.0p25` and the 6 alternating secondary levels (`875,825,775,725,675,625` hPa) through `pgrb2b.0p25` inventories and exact byte ranges; require unique grid/time/parameter/level/statistical-window matches, allowing only fully byte-identical duplicate messages as above. Disambiguate `.idx` entries that share variable/level/forecast labels (such as `APCP:surface`) and verify decoded Section 4 PDT 4.8 metadata before admission. SJ-202's wind/height proof does not itself prove every needed secondary `TMP` field: decode and admit those explicitly.

Dependencies/missingness are per layer: the two lift layers depend on the admitted thermal/surface inputs; PBL is required only for its closure branch. Rain depends only on admitted precipitation intervals and coverage. A cell missing flux/profile/humidity can still publish valid rain, and missing rain does not zero or invalidate lift. Rain never uses lift-support flags or `no-usable-lift`. Missing a configured required acquisition/frame fails publication, but a decoded per-cell bitmap missing value propagates only to its dependent layers.

No upper-air `RH`/`SPFH`/`DPT`, `U`/`V` winds, `VVEL`, `TCDC`, `CAPE` or cloud inputs in this dry method. Preserve source missing masks, below-terrain screening, height convention and unit conversions. Process one forecast frame at a time: decode one field at a time, compact only active-mask cells into a per-frame column buffer (`N_active × 21` `Float32` values for `HGT` and `TMP`), reuse static grid/terrain geometry, release full-grid `Float64Array` buffers immediately after each field, and never retain a full global 21-level cube across all eight frames. A geographic mask saves column/raster work, not the download/decode cost of a globally packed GRIB field.

Surface `SPFH` is kg/kg specific humidity, not dewpoint or mixing ratio: compute mixing ratio `r = q / (1 - q)` directly (or vapour pressure `e = p * r / (epsilon + r)` with consistent pressure units) and pass `r` directly into virtual temperature / virtual heat flux in `computeThermalVelocityMps`, or accept source 2 m `DPT` with K-to-C conversion and one unified saturation vapour pressure formulation (avoiding round-trip drift between Bolton and Alduchov-Eskridge Magnus constants). Pin the actual GFS/HRRR humidity selection, require explicit surface `PRES` (no silent `hypsometricSurfacePressureHpa` 1013.25 hPa fallback), reject invalid units/ranges, and test `SPFH`-vs-dewpoint equivalence. No silent dry-air approximation when required surface humidity is absent.

### Flux windows, precipitation and f000
Reuse `gfsWindowStart`/`trailingGfsValues` and PDT 4.8 GRIB statistical-interval validation (`readNoaaStatisticalInterval` / `admitNoaaWindowPair` with continuous `increment === 0`), rather than rewriting from labels. For means sharing start `a`, with times in seconds: `meanF(t1,t2) = ((t2 - a) * meanF(a,t2) - (t1 - a) * meanF(a,t1)) / (t2 - t1)`. This gives `2 * meanF(0,6) - meanF(0,3)` for 3–6 h. Later GFS six-hour blocks reset their start; `f009` is not automatically initialization-to-9 h. For ICON initialization means, use `a = 0`, then normalize the verified producer sign to positive upward. Retain source windows and predecessor records; reject mismatched starts, grids, runs, processing types or ambiguous reset semantics. Signed cooling flux is physical, not an error to clamp away.

`W*` uses admitted interval flux with the valid-time endpoint profile/PBL: record `fluxWindow`, `profileTime`, and this temporal approximation. HRRR retains its verified native flux statistic rather than relabelling an instantaneous/shorter product as a three-hour mean. Precipitation must cover the exact advertised interval: use admitted means, weighted contiguous shorter means, or compatible accumulation differences; no gaps, overlapping windows or incompatible resets. Convert `kg/m²/s` (or `kg/m²` over interval duration) to `mm/h`. In `trailingGfsValues` (and regional accumulation differences), replace the strict `recovered < 0 -> null` rejection with a documented GRIB quantization tolerance derived from Section 5 packing error (or a pinned bound such as `packingError_current + packingError_prev + 1e-9`, clamping tiny negative quantization noise within `[-tolerance, 0)` to `0` while rejecting larger negative differences).

GFS sample: eight frames `f003…f024` and eight consecutive three-hour rain intervals covering the first 24 forecast hours. Standard `pgrb2` `f000` lift and rain are unavailable, not required build outputs and not no-lift. No prior-cycle or Gaussian-grid flux substitution. Required acquisition/frame failures fail the build; legitimate per-cell missingness remains explicit.

### Method `dry-lenschow-pbl-1p2-v1`
Derive on coherent source columns before display reprojection. Use model terrain/pressure, decoded 2 m surface state, dry lapse rate `0.0098 K/m` and resolved positive-to-nonpositive parcel crossing. Preserve the reference helper's branch scope: PBL limits only the first-layer closure, not a resolved crossing or profile-top branch. A crossing above model PBL is not silently capped; missing PBL does not invalidate an otherwise resolved crossing. A profile-top result is explicitly top-limited/support-flagged, never extended beyond observed support. Equality at the surface anchor is not no-lift. ## 4. One precomputed coverage policy, not global GIS on every run
Policy `soaring-land-v1`: land/coastal coverage within `55°S–65°N`, 50 km seaward buffer, excluding `RUS`, `ATA` and `GRL`. Keep deserts and inland lakes included in v1; no subjective “uninhabited” exclusions. This deliberately excludes much of high-latitude Alaska/Canada and all Russia; expose that limitation rather than labelling it model unavailability. Natural Earth is public domain; pin the 1:50m admin-0 source release, checksum and attribute mapping (including exceptional ISO fields/territories).

A one-time `masks:build` command produces a mask manifest plus model/export-grid coverage bitsets and XYZ tile bitsets for supported zooms. Commit the small generated lookups and regeneration recipe under coverage data in `packages/contracts`; do not ship large vector geometry to every client. One-off GIS tooling may be Python; scheduled forecast builds do no polygon buffering or DEM work.

Generation rules: handle/split the antimeridian; use geodesic buffering or appropriate local metric projections, not one global LAEA or “50 km” in degrees. Apply latitude/exclusion clipping after buffering. Use conservative cell-footprint intersection to retain coast/island representation. Hard-excluded country footprints take precedence: cells intersecting an excluded polygon are output-inactive, even if this conservatively removes a border strip outside it. The output domain is the union of admitted nearest-cell footprints, clipped to the latitude band; 50 km is the source-geometry buffer, not a promise of an exact pixel coastline. At GFS spacing this can overreach the coastal band or underfill excluded borders by approximately a cell diagonal; disclose and inspect that approximation. Separate output bits from input-only interpolation halo bits: halo support never makes output active. Query coverage uses the nearest output cell plus latitude/domain checks; rendering uses the identical rule. Generate expected-tile bits from intersection with that output footprint, not tile-center tests. Verify Torrey Pines, Karioitahi, Big Sur, Azores, Canaries, Armenia, NZ, Aleutians and dateline cases; inspect tiny-island omissions before admitting the mask. If the source omits a required island, use an explicit versioned geometry correction, not an invisible runtime exception.

For a `1,440×721` GFS grid, a cell bitset is `129,780` bytes; `z0–5` has `1,365` addresses, so its bitset is `171` bytes. Declare bit ordering, grid axes, pole/wrap rules, zoom offsets and hash in the manifest. Regional meshes use their own precomputed native-column/output lookup derived from the same policy, not GFS array indices. Outside-domain columns beyond the 1-cell interpolation halo skip lift derivation and carry `outside-domain` status in numeric arrays; halo cells are derived solely so bilinear interpolation across the outer half-cell footprint of coastal output cells has valid neighbor support, while `isCovered` enforces the identical nearest-cell tie-break rule (`row` then wrapped `column` at `0.5`) so a halo cell never makes output active or serves as a nearest-neighbor fallback; zero-bit tiles are never rendered/archived.

Measure rather than assume: active cells/tiles by zoom, support-halo overhead, retained coastal coverage and observed build speed. Neither 83% CPU savings nor ~230 active tiles is an acceptance assumption; coastline intersections at low zoom and global decoding can materially change those numbers.

### Continuous scalar reconstruction, not blocky no-lift boundaries
Export to a declared regular `EPSG:4326` grid at source-appropriate resolution, without claiming it is the native mesh. For lift, permit interpolation across known valid and physical no-lift neighbors: no-lift contributes AGL `0` and model terrain as the internal MSL rendering reference. Positive reconstructed AGL yields an explicitly interpolated estimate; an all-no-lift result still exposes MSL `null`/AGL `0`. This preserves `MSL = terrain + AGL` internally without publishing terrain as usable lift. Never substitute zero for `unavailable`/`outside-domain`.

Exact sampler rule: first reject outside output coverage. Snap coordinates within `1e-10` grid steps of an integer grid index, wrap periodic longitude, and ignore zero-weight neighbors. Interpolate only if every nonzero-weight contributor is known (`valid`/`no-lift` for lift, `valid` for rain). Otherwise return the nearest contributing cell's value/status without interpolation or weight renormalization; ties choose the lower row then wrapped column index. If that nearest cell is `unavailable`/`outside`, return that mask; do not search for a valid substitute. Nonperiodic domain edges never extrapolate. At an exact grid node only that node is required. Union support only from actual contributors. Apply this rule in native-to-export mapping where applicable and in all export-grid consumers. Report interpolation/support branches; do not imply the scientific 1.2 m/s solver was rerun at a map pixel.

One shared sampler handles export, `/sample`, and rendering. For tile pixels use exact global Web Mercator coordinates and inverse-project each center/subpixel to lat/lon before sampling the `EPSG:4326` field. Use deterministic 2×2 coverage supersampling at fixed quarter-pixel offsets `(x + 0.25, y + 0.25), (x + 0.75, y + 0.25), (x + 0.25, y + 0.75), (x + 0.75, y + 0.75)`, palette each subpixel from its reconstructed scalar, average premultiplied RGBA, and encode straight alpha. No tile-local warp, gutters or spatial blur. Center scalar agreement and blended edge-pixel agreement are separate tests; blended RGB need not be a palette swatch. Test no-lift transitions, dateline, masks and tile borders.

### Numeric samples: layers are independent
Use one simple uncompressed row-major array object per layer/frame, not a hardcoded three-layer pack or sparse chunk index. Each cell is `Float32` little-endian value plus `UInt8` status/support (`5 bytes`); grid dimensions/axis/wrap/version live in the manifest. A fourth layer adds files, not a new parser. A `1,440×721` layer is `5,191,200` bytes: 24 arrays are about 125 MB/build before archive data. Accept this small dense baseline; do not implement sparse rank/offset tables until measured retention/read costs warrant it. Masks still eliminate expensive derivation/rendering; they do not promise smaller dense arrays.

The low two status bits encode `valid`/`no-lift`/`unavailable`/`outside-domain`; three independent flag bits record `parcel-crossing`/`profile-top`/`PBL-closure` contributors. Union support flags through reprojection and interpolation; compare status bits only for validity. No-lift MSL's internal value is terrain solely for reconstruction; public no-lift MSL stays `null`. All layout/flag constants come from contracts.

A bilinear sample needs adjacent cell pairs `(i, i+1)` in two consecutive rows `(j, j+1)`. Because a `1,440`-column row is only `7,200 bytes`, coalesce each layer/frame lookup into **a single contiguous R2 byte-range read** covering `(j * 7200 + i * 5)` through `((j + 1) * 7200 + (i + 1) * 5 + 4)` (`7,210 bytes` for interior longitudes, or `14,400 bytes` across both full rows when wrapping `i = 1439 -> 0`), slicing the four `5-byte` cells out of the returned buffer; validate byte counts and bounds. Shared static mask lookups return `outside-domain` with zero array reads. A full 8-frame × 3-layer `/sample` therefore requires at most **24 R2 range reads** (1 per layer/frame, comfortably below the 50-subrequest Worker ceiling) rather than 48–96 split reads; still bound concurrency (e.g., 4–6 in flight), deduplicate identical ranges, and deploy on Workers Paid for production headroom. JSON response caching and optional layer/frame selection reduce repeat work.

## 6. One bucket, immutable builds, one conditional pointer
```text
Node CLI / GitHub Actions
  acquire minimal ranges -> decode existing TS -> masked science -> PNG/PMTiles + per-layer samples
  -> validate locally -> upload immutable artifacts -> manifest last -> CAS latest pointer

Windy / preview / other clients -> one read-only Hono Worker
  metadata + bounded samples + decoded XYZ tiles -> Cache API / single private-bound R2 bucket
```
Object layout: `builds/{model}/{buildId}/{layer}/{frame}.pmtiles`, `.../samples/{layer}/{frame}.bin`, `.../manifest.json`; `catalog/{model}/latest.json`; versioned masks/palettes/static blank tile. Prefix paths need no content-addressed registry. Build ID never reused; manifest records cycle, input/tool/method/mask hashes, grid/coverage, intervals, artifacts/checksums, palettes and expiry.

Keep this one bucket private, with public `r2.dev`/custom-domain bypass disabled. Bind it to the Worker, whose allowlisted routes serve tiles/metadata/JSON samples only. Numeric data is not confidential, but there is no need for another public raw-storage surface or brittle WAF prefix protection. No raw model inputs, credentials or arbitrary object proxy routes. Batch write credentials are bucket-scoped secrets; Worker needs no provider credentials. Raw acquisition caches stay on runner/local disk, not a new archive product.

### Publication steps
1. Read current model pointer and ETag before publishing. Reject an older cycle; same-cycle method replacement requires explicit intent. Build into a unique local/output prefix; no heartbeat or remote job state.
2. Verify PMTiles structure and expected active tile coverage, scientific/status summaries, arrays, exact time intervals and checksums. Upload immutable artifacts once with create-only semantics. Upload a complete immutable manifest last and verify referenced objects/range reads. Never mutate an existing build.
3. Atomically write `catalog/{model}/latest.json` with captured `If-Match`, or `If-None-Match:*` for first publish. Pointer includes latest manifest and its retained-build list (maximum 128 entries; refuse admission rather than silently dropping an unexpired entry), avoiding a separate catalog transaction. This CAS is admission: every build-specific route checks retained membership and expiry before blank-tile handling or Worker cache lookup, using the short-lived per-model pointer cache (`Map<ModelId, Cached<ModelPointer>>`). If cached membership misses, refresh that model's authoritative pointer once before returning 404: another instance may have just discovered the newly committed build. Never negative-cache such a miss beyond that check. A complete manifest from a losing CAS is not publicly admitted. On 412, abort; do not blindly refresh ETag and overwrite the winner. An explicit rerun rereads and rechecks cycle ordering.
4. A failed/killed build leaves `latest` untouched. Build IDs contain creation time and are never resurrected after the bounded publisher lifetime: enforce at most six hours from ID creation through final CAS in CLI and runner, including after suspension/retry. Reuse identical verified partial artifacts only within that lifetime; otherwise use a fresh ID. Unreferenced partial objects are cleaned later. No custom checkpoint-resume framework.
5. Rollback: pause scheduling, cancel/wait for active publishers, run the same serialized CLI workflow to CAS an existing verified retained manifest. Resume publication explicitly. CAS protects overlapping writes; GitHub concurrency alone is not a distributed correctness guarantee.
6. Keep archives and sample arrays/manifests/masks for seven days and protect current/explicit rollback targets. Cleanup uses the same per-model Actions serialization; manual cleanup/rollback requires pausing other publisher entrypoints and waiting for active jobs. Only delete unreferenced builds at least 24 hours old, well beyond the enforced six-hour publisher lifetime; never resurrect such IDs. Retire expired entries with pointer CAS, reread retained references before deletion, and protect shared masks/palettes still referenced. Rollback may choose only a currently retained target, never an orphan scheduled for deletion. Fresh concurrent local builds remain protected by age; test this rather than asserting an invisible active-job ledger. No bucket lifecycle rule may independently delete referenced data. Cached delivery and origin retention are separate: clients honor expiry, and TTL rules below prevent a cached view being treated as current after expiry.

Scheduling: GitHub Actions schedule off the top of the hour plus `workflow_dispatch`, model concurrency group with `cancel-in-progress:false`, bounded timeout/retries, and the same `pnpm build:sample --model gfs` CLI usable locally. Discovery selects a complete admissible upstream run, not a guessed publication lag. GitHub schedules may delay/drop or be disabled; expose stale data and monitor freshness, with manual rerun or the same CLI on one cron runner as the fallback—not another orchestration service. Start one cycle/day; four/day and regional runs are measured configuration changes.

### Cache behavior that actually exists
Worker returns extracted PNG as 200, then stores that full response in `caches.default` under a canonical immutable XYZ URL. Never try to cache R2 range responses as 206. Reuse bounded per-isolate PMTiles header/directory caches keyed by immutable archive identity; no whole-archive download. Cache API is per-POP, not automatically Tiered Cache; every request still invokes a Worker and cold tiles may incur multiple R2 reads. Set response headers in the Worker, not merely on archive objects.

Build-bound tiles/manifests/samples use `max-age=min(configuredImmutableTTL, floor(expiresAt - now))`, never below zero, with ETag; cap Worker cache storage/returned client TTL at the same deadline. Recheck admission/expiry before Worker cache hits and recompute remaining client TTL on return; no stale-serving directive may extend expiry. Immutable globally versioned palettes/masks may retain a long TTL. Already browser-cached bytes are not revocable, so clients also stop assembling an expired build. Pointer/latest uses a short per-model cache (approximately 15-second reader); freshness/age is explicit. Generated samples use normalized validated query keys. Do not long-cache missing/future resources, 5xx or unexpected archive holes. Set CORS/MIME/GET/HEAD behavior, reject arbitrary query/cache-key fragmentation and add sampling abuse limits. Measure cold and warm paths separately; no “near-zero cost” or one-read-per-cold-tile promise.

## 7. Small reusable API, no discovery waterfall

One layer registry owns IDs, units, fields, palette, sample interpretation and artifact links. No separately handwritten endpoint or legend manual.

- `GET /v1/catalog`: supported models, actual coverage/capabilities, latest links and freshness; per-model failure is visible.
- `GET /v1/models/{model}/latest?expand=manifest`: pointer plus optional complete inline manifest, frame/layer tile templates, bounds, `maxNativeZoom`, palette summaries and coverage-mask links/compact tile bits. A client can request tiles after one HTTP round trip. Pin returned build throughout the view.
